# Examples

`example-workspace/` is a small, complete, entirely fictional workspace. Read
it before setting up your own; one filled-in example makes the templates
obvious in a way the templates themselves do not.

Everything in it is invented: the people, the project, the grant, the journal.
Nothing here corresponds to anyone real.

## What to look at, in order

1. **`AGENTS.md`** at the root. This is the file that does the work. Note how
   specific the rules are, and that the confidentiality section names actual
   categories rather than saying "be careful".

2. **`projects/2026-coastal-erosion/`**. A unit with the full contract. Read
   the three files together and notice the division of labour:

   - `README.md` says what it is and, crucially, that the manuscript lives in
     Overleaf. The folder is the private layer, not the source.
   - `STATUS.md` fits on a screen and has one next action with an owner.
   - `LOG.md` records why the model was changed, including the reversal of an
     earlier decision. That reasoning is not recoverable from the files
     themselves.

3. **`areas/nordblue-consortium/`**. An area rather than a project: no
   completion state, so `STATUS.md` tracks a rhythm instead of progress toward
   an end.

4. **`people/mira-lindqvist.md`**. A single note, no contract. Not every unit
   needs three files.

## What it does not show

Scale. A real workspace has dozens of units and an `archive/` with years in
it. Two units and an area is enough to see the pattern, and a realistic
example would be mostly noise.

It also shows a healthy workspace. Yours will have half-activated units for a
long time, which is normal. See the adoption notes in
[`../docs/the-method.md`](../docs/the-method.md#known-weaknesses).
